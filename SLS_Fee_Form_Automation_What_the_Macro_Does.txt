SLS Fee Form Automation: What the Macro Does

Oct 18, 2026 · @Louisa

Overview

The macro runs inside Outlook, reads every email with "SLS Fee Form" in the subject (or in the name of a PDF attachment), copies the fee details from the PDF into an Excel log, and emails a weekly summary of the fees, split into Upfront Fees, Amortised Fees and Accounts Payable. It does this on its own each weekday, as long as Outlook is open.

The daily sweep

The sweep runs at 8:30 on weekdays and again whenever Outlook starts, so a late start still catches up. For each new fee form email it:

1.  Saves the PDF with the email's received date and time in the file name.

2.  Opens the PDF in Word and reads the To: line (Segment), form date, borrower, deal details and Syndications OIC/2IC.

3.  Reads each fee line. A box holding two currencies and amounts becomes two log lines, with every other column repeated.

4.  Writes the rows to the Excel log. Each fee line is given a fee type in the Status column, based on the first digit of its GL account: 5 = "Upfront Fees", 4 = "Amortised Fees", 2 = "Accounts Payable". GST lines are marked "GST", and any other GL is marked "Unclassified GL".

5.  Checks each currency's lines against the form total and notes any mismatch or unreadable field in the Check column.

6.  Tags the email "SLS Logged", or "SLS Review" if a check failed, so it is never processed twice.

The weekly digest

The digest is emailed on Mondays at 9:00 to the recipients in the settings. If Outlook opens late, it sends at startup instead.

-   Scope: fee forms dated this month onwards, grouped by month.

-   Fee types: GL starting with 5 is shown under Upfront Fees, 4 under Amortised Fees, and 2 under Accounts Payable. The same rule sets the Status column in the Excel log, and it is applied to the GL account each time the digest runs, so older log rows follow it too.

-   Order: Upfront Fees first, then Amortised Fees, then Accounts Payable, each grouped by month with per-currency totals.

-   Amounts: shown exactly as they appear on the fee forms. Accounts Payable fees are not reduced when a later form books them as Upfront or Amortised.

-   Subject: "SLS fee form summary (Mmm yyyy onwards) - Upfront: X | Amortised: Y | In AP: Z".

-   Sending: sent automatically, and skipped when there is nothing to report.

-   Not shown: GST lines are left out. If a fee line has a GL that does not start with 2, 4 or 5, the email adds a note asking you to check the "Unclassified GL" rows in the log.

-   Link: the email ends with a link to the Excel log.

What it touches

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Area                What the macro does
  ------------------- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mailbox             Reads the chosen folder and adds a category to processed emails. It never deletes, moves or replies.

  Your PC             Saves PDFs to the PDF folder, starts hidden Word and Excel, adds two calendar reminders as a scheduler, and sets one Word user-profile setting.

  Excel log           Adds rows to the log file and links each row to its source PDF.

  Outgoing email      Sends only the digest, only to the recipients in the settings.

  Network             Makes no outside connections beyond opening the log if it is a SharePoint address.
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Limits and what to tell IT or risk

-   Outlook must be open, and macros must be enabled in the Trust Center. It does not run in the New Outlook.

-   It depends on the current form layout. A layout change can break the reading.

-   Reading a PDF through Word can get a field wrong, so rows marked "SLS Review" or with a Check note need a manual look.

-   The macro handles client deal data, so tell IT or risk before relying on it.
